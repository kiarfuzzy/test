Bed Adhesion Problems and How to Fix Them

Almost every bed adhesion problem falls into one of four categories. Find your symptom below rather than changing settings at random.

6 min read

A good first layer is squished slightly flat, fully opaque, and consistent across the whole bed. Most adhesion problems trace back to one of: leveling, temperature, surface condition, or a mismatch between material and surface — and the symptom usually tells you which.

Corners lifting or curling up (warping)

This is a cooling and shrinkage problem, most common with ABS and ASA, and occasionally PETG on large flat parts. As the bottom layers cool faster than the top of the print, the plastic contracts unevenly and pulls the corners up off the bed.

  • Increase bed temperature — warping filaments usually want a hotter bed than the printer's default (see the range table on the Temperature page).
  • Use an enclosure, or at minimum eliminate drafts, to keep ambient temperature more consistent around the print.
  • Add a brim — a few extra millimeters of single-layer plastic around the base — to increase how much surface area is holding the part down.
  • Reduce cooling fan speed for the first several layers if your slicer profile has it cranked up.

Entire print detaches partway through

This usually means the first layer never really bonded in the first place, even if it looked okay at a glance.

  • Re-level the bed. This is the single most common fix — most modern printers have an assisted or automatic leveling routine; run it before assuming anything else is wrong.
  • Check your Z-offset (nozzle height above the bed for the first layer). Too high and the filament doesn't get pressed into the bed at all; too low and it can scrape or clog.
  • Clean the bed surface with isopropyl alcohol — oils from handling the bed (even just fingerprints) can meaningfully reduce adhesion.

Patchy first layer — sticks in some spots, not others

This is almost always a leveling problem where the bed (or the gantry) isn't quite parallel to the nozzle's travel plane, so the nozzle-to-bed distance changes across the print area.

  • Re-level at multiple points, not just the center — a bed can be level at the middle and still be off at the corners.
  • If your printer has bed mesh compensation, run and apply a fresh mesh — this maps out small dips and bumps across the bed rather than assuming it's perfectly flat.
  • Check the bed surface itself for damage or a worn coating in the affected spots.
Match the surface to the material. Smooth PEI holds PLA well but can be too slick for some materials without help. A glue stick or dedicated adhesive works as a reliable middle ground across most materials and surfaces if you're not sure what your bed is tuned for.

General surface prep that helps almost everything

  1. Wipe the bed with isopropyl alcohol (90%+ concentration) before prints that matter — this removes oils without leaving residue.
  2. Re-level periodically, especially after moving the printer or swapping the bed surface.
  3. Print a first layer at a slightly lower speed than the rest of the print — most slicer profiles already do this, and it noticeably helps consistency.
  4. For consistently tricky materials, a brim is a low-effort, reliable first thing to try before adjusting anything else.
